# [Parameter Estimation] Multi-Interference Source Radar Signal Amplitude and Phase Estimation Based on Fully Convolutional Networks [Attached Code]

Introduction: To reduce the number of traffic accidents and road fatalities, autonomous driving and road safety are crucial topics. One solution proposed by car companies for building autonomous and safer vehicles is based on using radar sensors to scan the surrounding environment. The most commonly used radar sensor in the automotive industry is Frequency Modulated Continuous Wave (FMCW) / Linear Frequency Modulation Sequence (CS) radar, which transmits linear frequency modulated signal sequences. The signals transmitted and received by such sensors provide means to estimate the distance and speed of nearby targets (e.g., vehicles, pedestrians, or other obstacles). For example, radar sensors have even been used to detect very small objects (e.g., road debris [1]). However, the widespread use of radar sensors [2] increases the possibility of interference between different vehicle sensors, leading to damage and unavailable signals. In fact, radio frequency interference can significantly increase the noise floor, reaching a level where potential targets are completely masked by noise, thereby reducing the sensitivity of target detection methods [3]. In Figure 1, we present the distance profiles of the interfered and uninterrupted radar signals, where some targets visible in the clean distance profile are absorbed by the rising background noise caused by multiple interference sources. To be able to detect such targets, it is necessary to mitigate radar interference. For this purpose, researchers have proposed various techniques, including traditional methods [4]-[11] to deep learning methods [12]-[16].
In this paper, we extend our previous work [12] by designing a novel Fully Convolutional Network (FCN) [17] that: (i) can recover the phase of radar differential backscatter signals along with their amplitudes, and (ii) can handle multiple non-coherent RF interference sources. Our network serves as an input to the real part, imaginary part, and short-time Fourier transform (STFT) of the interference signals, providing outputs for the real part, imaginary part, and distance images respectively. Although our network does not directly estimate the phase, it can be easily calculated from the real and imaginary parts. To the best of our knowledge, we are among the few who have proposed deep learning models capable of accurately estimating phase, a well-known problem often addressed in related articles as future work [18]. While most deep learning methods have focused on radar interference mitigation from individual interference sources [11], [12], [14], our goal is to address tasks under multiple interference sources. To achieve this, we generated a large-scale dataset that closely replicates real car scenes with multiple interference sources during training, considering up to three interference sources during training and up to six interference sources during inference. We compared our method with three state-of-the-art approaches, one based on zeroing [4], [9], two based on deep neural networks [12], [13], reporting superior results across various evaluation metrics. In this paper, we also applied weight pruning [19], [20] to enhance the signal-to-noise ratio in the neural network model weights. We compared our weight pruning with widely adopted dropout [21] to show that the former method helps neural models reach better convergence points. Moreover, we released our new dataset as open-source, allowing other researchers and engineers to objectively compare their future work in radar interference mitigation. In summary, our contributions are threefold:
·We propose a deep learning model capable of mitigating non-coherent RF interference from multiple sources.
· We designed a fully convolutional network architecture that outputs clear distance profiles, estimates amplitudes, and indirect phases.  
· We introduced a radar interference dataset with a wide and realistic range of signal parameter variations and multiple sources of interference.  
Article illustrations  
Conclusion This paper presents a novel fully convolutional network that can simultaneously estimate the amplitudes and phases of automotive radar signals under the influence of multiple interference sources. We also introduced a large-scale radar signal database to simulate real-world and complex settings. In a series of comprehensive experiments, we compared our FCN model with some state-of-the-art methods, showing that our FCN model provides superior results. We also released our new dataset for future work to facilitate objective comparisons. To the best of our knowledge, we are the first to establish a benchmark dataset for automotive radar interference mitigation with multiple interference sources. In future work, our goal is to modify our FCN or explore model extraction methods to enable real-time processing on low-cost embedded devices. Currently, real-time processing is only possible on expensive GPUs.
